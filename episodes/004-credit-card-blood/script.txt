Every seven days, without tasting it or seeing it, you take in about a plastic credit card's worth of microplastics.

Sounds impossible, right? We've all seen the heartbreaking photos: sea turtles tangled in plastic ghost nets, and endless mountains of trash choking our coastlines.

We thought all that plastic stayed out in the ocean. It didn't.

Ocean waves and ultraviolet sunlight slowly crush that waste into microscopic fragments. First microplastics, then nanoplastics—particles so unimaginably small they can slip through barriers and enter the bloodstream.

Scientists now report synthetic polymers in human lungs, hearts, and even brain tissue. That is about five grams of plastic every single week. Two hundred and fifty grams a year. By age seventy, some estimates put lifetime intake near twenty kilograms of industrial polymers.

And here is the terrifying realization: we didn't just turn the oceans into a landfill. We turned ourselves into one.

You can't clean the planet overnight. But avoiding single-use plastics and filtering your drinking water can cut a major intake path.
